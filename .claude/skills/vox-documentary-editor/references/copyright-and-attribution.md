# Copyright, Licensing & Attribution

You may research, view, analyse and study copyrighted photographs and illustrations as visual
reference. **Educational purpose alone does not authorise reuse.** Classification and record-
keeping are mandatory production steps, not legal advice — flag anything uncertain for human
review rather than deciding it yourself.

## Classification

Every asset gets exactly one class in `ASSETS.md`:

- **A — Public domain.** Expired term, US federal government work, formally dedicated (CC0).
  Verify the specific basis and record it; term rules differ by country and by publication date.
- **B — Openly licensed.** CC BY, CC BY-SA, open-content programmes, permissive museum licences.
  Record the exact licence version and comply with its conditions — including share-alike
  implications for the work it is embedded in.
- **C — Licensed or permission granted.** You hold a licence or written permission. Record
  what it covers (medium, territory, duration) and store the evidence path.
- **D — Possibly covered by an applicable exception**, subject to case-specific review
  (e.g. quotation, criticism, news reporting, or fair use factors in the US). This class is
  **not self-certifying**: mark it `PENDING-REVIEW`, note the specific argument and the amount
  used, and surface it to the user for a decision. Never promote D to APPROVED on your own.
- **E — Reference-only.** Study it; do not ship it.

## Working from reference-only material

Permitted: analyse the composition, lighting, subject arrangement, information design and
colour logic; understand *why* it works; then produce an independent original that solves the
same communication problem.

Not permitted: tracing it; running it through a filter or style transfer; recolouring it;
cropping it and calling it new; recreating it shot-for-shot as a near-identical derivative.

The test: could an informed viewer place your graphic and the reference side by side and
identify yours as a copy? If yes, start again from the underlying information.

## For material you intend to actually use

1. Identify the licence or legal basis, from the rights-holder or archive's own statement.
2. Record creator, title, source, date, licence, licence URL, and the required attribution string.
3. Satisfy attribution requirements — on screen, in credits, or in the description, as the
   licence requires. CC BY requires attribution *with the work*; a description-only credit may
   not satisfy it for a video.
4. Use only the amount justified by the purpose.
5. Honour no-derivatives and non-commercial terms. An ND asset cannot be cropped, graded,
   cut out or composited.
6. Treat share-alike carefully: a CC BY-SA asset may impose licence conditions on the whole film.
7. Flag anything unresolved; do not render it into a master.

## Also check, beyond copyright

Trademarks (logos, flags with statutory restrictions, product marks) · personality and
publicity rights for identifiable living people · privacy and dignity for images of victims,
patients, children, the dead · museum and archive terms of use that restrict commercial reuse
even of public-domain scans · music and sound-effect licences, which are separate from image
licences · platform-specific clearance.

## Credits output

Produce `out/CREDITS.md` and an on-screen credit roll generated **from `ASSETS.md`**, so the
two can never drift. Include every B and C asset, every A asset whose archive requests
courtesy credit, and a line distinguishing original graphics from archival material.

## Absolute prohibitions

Never fabricate a licence, a licence URL, a creator name, a permission, an attribution or a
source. Never write "public domain" without the basis. Never state that material is cleared
when it is `PENDING-REVIEW`. Never present a recreation, colourisation, AI-generated image or
composite as authentic archival footage — label it on screen.
