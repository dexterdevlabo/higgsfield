# Child-safety and age banding

Educational content for children carries obligations ordinary explainers do not. A child
cannot evaluate a source; they will simply believe the video.

## Declare the age band first

Nothing is storyboarded before this is fixed. It changes every other decision.

| | 3–5 | 6–9 | 10–12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sentence length | 5–8 words | 8–14 words | 12–20 words |
| Words per minute | ~100 | ~130 | ~150 |
| Ideas per film | 1 | 1–2 | 2–3 |
| Min dwell on readable text | 2.5s | 1.8s | 1.4s |
| New vocabulary | avoid; name things plainly | 1 new word, defined on screen | 2–3, defined |
| Numbers | "lots", "a few" | up to 100, counted visually | real figures with units |
| Cut rhythm | slow, 3–5s shots | 2–4s | 1.8–3s |
| Music | gentle, sparse | light rhythm | fuller |

**Dwell is the one most often got wrong.** An adult short holds text for 1.2s. A six-year-old
reading at ~60 wpm needs more than double that. Compute it: `0.6s + 0.25s per word`, floored
at the band's minimum.

## Content constraints

- **No peril.** No character in danger, no chase, no threat, no darkness used as menace,
  no "what happens next could be terrible".
- **No jump scares and no sudden loud transients.** Keep the mix peaks gentle; children often
  wear headphones. Target a quieter master than the adult spec — around −18 LUFS — and avoid
  sub impacts under 60 Hz.
- **No engagement bait.** No "wait for it", no false cliffhanger, no countdown pressure, no
  "99% of kids get this wrong". These work on children precisely because they are manipulative.
- **No photorealistic children, and no real child's likeness.** Use stylised or illustrated
  characters. Put this in the image prompt explicitly, and reject a generation that drifts
  photoreal.
- **No brands, logos, or commercial characters** in generated imagery.
- **Nothing that shames.** Not getting something is normal; the film should say so.

## Accuracy, which matters more here, not less

The parent skill's rules apply unchanged: verify at a primary source, never invent a
statistic, state uncertainty where it exists. Two additions for children:

- **Simplify without falsifying.** "The Earth goes round the Sun once a year" is a fair
  simplification. "Dinosaurs died because of one asteroid, the end" states a contested detail
  as settled. Prefer a true simple sentence to a tidy false one.
- **Say when nobody knows.** "Scientists are still working this out" is a good line for a
  child to hear, and it is often the honest one.

## Platform and legal

- **YouTube "Made for Kids" is a COPPA compliance flag, not a genre tag.** Setting it disables
  comments, personalised ads, notifications and some playback features, and misdeclaring
  carries real penalties. The uploader must set it; tell them before they publish.
- Instagram and TikTok have their own minimum-age terms and restrict some features on
  content aimed at minors. The account holder is responsible; flag it, don't advise on it.
- **Captions always.** Muted viewing is the norm, and on-screen text supports early reading.

## Generated-asset disclosure

Illustrated art generated by a model is a third asset class alongside verified archival and
code-drawn graphics. Record it in `ASSETS.md` with the model and prompt, so the film's
provenance stays auditable — the same standard the other films in this repo hold to.
