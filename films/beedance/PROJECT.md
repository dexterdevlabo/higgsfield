## Why this topic
It is a real scientific discovery, it is genuinely surprising to an adult let alone a child,
and — the part that matters for the budget — **most of it is a diagram**. The dance, the
angle, the sun, the distance are all code-drawn for free. Generated art is needed only to
establish three places: the meadow, the dark hive, and a bee on the comb.

## Fact check

| Claim on screen | Verified | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Bees dance to tell the hive where food is | yes | NC State Extension, *Honey Bee Dance Language* |
| The dance is a figure-of-eight on vertical comb, in the dark | yes | same |
| Angle of the waggle run **from vertical** = angle of food **from the sun's azimuth** | yes | NC State Extension; The Apiarist |
| Straight up = fly toward the sun | yes | same |
| Longer waggle = further away | yes | same |
| Karl von Frisch worked it out; Nobel Prize **1973** | yes | Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 1973, shared |

**Deliberately not stated:** the common "1 second of waggle ≈ 1 km" conversion. It varies by
subspecies, so the script says "the longer she waggles, the further you go" instead — true
without a caveat a child cannot evaluate.

**Also avoided:** calling the sun reference an "elevation" or "altitude" angle. One source
conflates these; the bee's reference is the sun's **compass bearing** (azimuth). The film
says "toward the sun", which is correct and age-appropriate.
